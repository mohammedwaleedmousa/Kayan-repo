# Kayan — standing instructions

## Arabic is the source language, not a translation
Arabic copy for Kayan is **authored in Arabic**, never translated from an English draft.
If a line reads like it was thought in English first, it is wrong. Rewrite it.

**Register:** Arabic administrative / contracting register — the language of لوائح، عقود، محاضر استلام.
Not marketing Arabic, not journalistic Arabic, not literary Arabic.

**Rules:**
- Lead with the verb, usually passive: «تودع»، «يصرف»، «تراجع»، «لا يجوز»، «يترتب على».
- Compact sentences. Arabic admin writing does not explain itself twice.
- Avoid the dual (المثنى) when it turns stiff — recast instead. Bad: «عملان موثقان وهذه خطوات استكمالهما». Good: «إرفاق عملين سابقين وجهة تزكية».
- No تشكيل anywhere.
- No «تم + مصدر» (translationese). Use the passive verb: «قُبل الطلب» not «تم قبول الطلب».
- No exclamation marks, no hype, no em-dash rhythm carried over from English.
- Use the industry's real terms: حساب الضمان، محضر الاستلام، أمر تغيير، نطاق العمل، المستحق، جهة تزكية، قرار مسبب، لجنة الالتزام.
- English lines are a **secondary** register, set smaller and lighter. They restate; they never lead.

**Test before shipping any Arabic line:** could this sentence appear, unedited, in a real Yemeni or Gulf contract, regulation, or bank notice? If no, rewrite.

## Never claim unverified facts
Any factual claim about Yemen, Aden, species, institutions, or dates must be checked against a
credible source and cited in the document. No invented heritage, no invented endemism.

## Tagline
«خلف كل نجاح، كيان» — *Behind every success, a Kayan.* Use verbatim.
