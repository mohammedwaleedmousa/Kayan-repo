3. What Kayan is — the settled position
Kayan is impact sourcing for professional work, built in the hardest operating environment in the world.
Expanded: Kayan is a verification institution monetised through managed delivery. It establishes, evidences and stands behind the competence of professionals in a constrained market, so that their work can be bought with confidence anywhere. The verification and delivery record is the compounding asset. Managed delivery is how that asset is built and paid for.
It is not a marketplace, not an agency, not a training provider, not an NGO, and not a recruitment firm. Each of those confusions has a specific and predictable cost, set out in K-00-01 §3.2.
3.1 The economic engine
The Absorption Flywheel. Sponsor capital funds Forge cohorts and civic programmes. Those produce verified professionals who enter the registry at no acquisition cost to the commercial line. Managed Delivery sells that verified capacity and earns a fee. Delivery produces revenue and evidence. The evidence — accepted deliverables, earnings records, the Absorption Ratio — is what unlocks the next sponsor and convinces the next client. Each turn lowers acquisition cost and raises credential value. The Hub is the physical anchor of the loop and is itself a revenue line.

9. The next session
Objective: make money movable. Nothing else in the queue matters until this is done.
Establish the treasury architecture. Determine where Rail A sits, what the bridge is, and what it costs. This requires two external inputs: counsel on the entity question, and a banking enquiry in the candidate jurisdictions.
Write the escrow operating procedure. What counts as proof of payment, who verifies it, what dual approval means in practice, what happens when funds arrive in the wrong account, how the escrow ledger reconciles to the bank, and how a daily cash close works at the Hub and café.
Build the contributor rate card, from observed local rates, by role and tier and specialisation.
Run the working capital analysis: when client money arrives, when contributors must be paid, and what the gap costs.
Then, and only then, the Managed Delivery operating standard and the contract set.
Everything else — platform, Hub, Forge, marketing, website — waits. This is not caution. It is the recognition that a company which cannot safely take money and pay people has no product, however good its documents are. [JUDGEMENT]
