1. Why this document exists
Every operational failure in a company of this kind traces back to the same root: the same person, organisation or piece of work exists in several places under several names, and nobody can say which is true. Reporting decays, money goes to the wrong account, and trust — the actual product — becomes unverifiable.
This document prevents that by settling four things before anything is built: what objects exist, how each is uniquely and permanently identified, how they relate to each other, and what happens automatically when something changes.
It follows a single design law, stated once and applied throughout: one real-world thing, one record, one identifier, one page. Everything else is a view.

3. The identity and numbering standard
3.1 Design requirements
An identifier in this operating environment must satisfy conditions that a simple database key does not. It will be written by hand, read over a poor phone line, typed into a bank transfer form, and quoted in a contract. It must therefore be:
Short enough to be said aloud and written without error
Self-validating, so that a single mistyped character is detected immediately rather than creating a wrong payment
Non-sequential in appearance to a casual observer, so it does not disclose volume to competitors or clients
Permanent — never reissued, never reused, never changed when a person&apos;s details change
Human-readable as to type, so that anyone can see at a glance whether they are looking at a person, an organisation or a piece of work
3.2 The format
KY-<TYPE>-<YY>-<SEQ>-<C>
Segment
Meaning
Rule
KY
Institution prefix
Fixed. Distinguishes a Kayan identifier from any other reference in a document.
TYPE
Entity type
T Talent · C Client · P Pod · E Engagement · S Sponsor · G Programme · M Member
YY
Year of issue
Two digits of the calendar year of first issue. Never changes thereafter.
SEQ
Sequence
Five digits, zero-padded, allocated in order within type and year. Resets each year.
C
Check character
Calculated. Detects any single-character error and most transpositions.
Examples: KY-T-26-00148-9 (a professional), KY-C-26-00037-7 (a client organisation), KY-P-26-00012-1 (a pod), KY-E-26-00205-1 (an engagement). Each check character below is the true value produced by the algorithm in 3.3 and may be verified by hand.
3.3 The check character
Calculated on the seven digits formed by YY followed by SEQ, using a modulus-11 weighted sum. This is deliberately simple enough to be verified by hand at a bank counter and strong enough to catch the errors that actually occur.
Take the seven digits left to right and multiply them by the weights 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2 respectively.
Add the seven products together.
Divide the total by 11 and take the remainder.
Subtract the remainder from 11. If the result is 11, the check character is 0. If the result is 10, the check character is X. Otherwise it is the result itself.
Worked example for year 26, sequence 00148: digits 2 6 0 0 1 4 8; products 16, 42, 0, 0, 4, 12, 16; sum 90; 90 modulo 11 is 2; 11 minus 2 is 9. The identifier is KY-T-26-00148-9.
Any system accepting a Kayan identifier must validate the check character before accepting the entry. An identifier that fails validation is rejected at the point of entry, never stored and corrected later. [RULE]
3.4 The public verification reference
The internal identifier is an operational key and is not published. Where a professional&apos;s verified status is shown publicly or shared with a client, a separate reference is used, derived from the same sequence but carrying no year and no internal meaning: KVR-00148.
The two are linked one to one and never interchanged. This separation allows the public reference to be displayed, shared and eventually recognised externally without exposing internal volumes, sequencing or record structure. [DECISION]
Whether the public reference is issued at launch or held until a delivery record exists is open question Q-01 in the Constitution.
3.5 Issuance and lifecycle rules
Rule
Detail
Issued once
At the moment the record is created, before any verification. A person at tier T0 already has a permanent identifier.
Never reused
A withdrawn, duplicated or fraudulent record retains its identifier permanently in a closed state. The number is never given to anyone else.
Never changed
Name changes, tier changes, specialisation changes and status changes do not alter the identifier.
Duplicate handling
Where two records are found to be the same person, one is designated primary; the other is marked merged, retains its number, and permanently redirects to the primary.
Quoted everywhere
The identifier appears on every contract, payment instruction, assessment result, reference request and delivery note relating to that entity.

4. The pod model
A pod is the unit of delivery. Kayan does not sell individuals; it sells a team with a named accountable lead. The pod is what makes the fee defensible and what makes delivery survivable when one person becomes unavailable — which, in this operating environment, is a certainty rather than a risk.
4.1 The three formation types
All three are valid, all three receive a pod identifier and a pod page, and all three deliver under the same standard. They differ in origin and in how the internal split is agreed.

A registered pod does not gain preferential access to work. Formation type is an administrative fact, not a status. Work allocation follows the same rule as verification: fit, availability and client requirement. [RULE — extends Constitution R2]

4.3 Pod rules
A person may belong to several pods and hold different roles in each.
A pod has exactly one lead at any time. A change of lead is an event on the pod record.
A pod cannot deliver an engagement unless every member is verified in the specialisations that engagement requires.
Where a member is drawn from a registered pod into a Kayan-composed pod, the releasing pod&apos;s consent is recorded before assignment.
A pod that completes an engagement retains its record and its history whether or not it delivers again.
Pod quality is measured at pod level and at member level. A weak pod does not conceal a strong member, and a strong pod does not launder a weak one.

5. Relationship pages
A relationship page is the single place where everything Kayan knows about one entity is held and where its relationship with Kayan visibly accumulates. It is not a profile and not a form output. It is the institutional memory of that relationship, and it is what allows a new staff member to pick up a five-year-old relationship in ten minutes.
Every page shares four structural properties: it is generated automatically at record creation, it is updated by events rather than by manual editing, it separates what the entity can see from what only Kayan can see, and it carries a complete chronological timeline that is never edited or deleted.

The operational heart of delivery: client, pod, scope of work, milestones with dates and status, escrow position, submissions and acceptances, change requests, communications, risks raised, delivery notes, acceptance certificates, payouts made, and the complete timeline. This is the page that a dispute is resolved from, and it must therefore be complete without reference to anyone&apos;s memory.

6.1 What this buys
Of twenty events, thirteen require no human at all and four require a human only at the moment of genuine judgement — verifying an identity, awarding a tier, authorising money, or resolving a dispute. Registration, testing, reference collection, reminders, expiry, record-writing and page updates are all machine work.
The consequence is direct and measurable. At one hundred registrations the difference between this design and a manual process is convenience. At one thousand it is the difference between a functioning institution and a backlog that destroys the credibility of the verification asset. The design assumption is ten thousand. [JUDGEMENT]

7. Data quality rules
#
Rule
DQ1
A record is never created without a validated identifier.
DQ2
Free text is not used where a controlled list can be used. Controlled lists are held centrally and referenced, never retyped.
DQ3
A field is captured once, at the point where the person who knows the answer is present, and never re-asked.
DQ4
Every record carries created-by, created-on, last-changed-by and last-changed-on. No exceptions.
DQ5
Nothing is deleted. Records are closed, merged or superseded, with a reason and a date.
DQ6
Every personal data field has a stated purpose, a retention period and a specific consent recorded against it.
DQ7
Identity documents are retained only as long as needed to complete verification; thereafter the verification result and a document hash are retained, not the image, unless a specific legal obligation requires otherwise.
DQ8
Any figure appearing in a report traces to a record. No figure is entered directly into a report.
