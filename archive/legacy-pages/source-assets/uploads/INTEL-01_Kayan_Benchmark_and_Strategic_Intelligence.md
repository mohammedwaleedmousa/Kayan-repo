# KAYAN — Benchmark & Strategic Intelligence Brief
### The homework behind the build: who leads this space, what Kayan takes, and where Kayan stands alone
**Type:** strategic intelligence (governance layer) · **Status:** living (refresh per Protocol §8) · **Purpose:** so every Kayan document is written from knowledge, not assertion.

## 1. Kayan's category has a name — and a lineage

What Kayan's **Managed Delivery** line actually is, in industry terms, is **impact sourcing**: intentionally employing talented people from underserved or disadvantaged communities to deliver professional/digital services to paying clients. This matters strategically: it gives Kayan an established, credible category with global precedent, recognised standards, and donor familiarity — instead of having to explain a novel idea from zero.

- **Pioneers & precedent:** Sama (Samasource), Digital Divide Data, iMerit, CloudFactory — all built businesses employing disadvantaged talent for digital work. The founding ethos across the field — *talent is evenly distributed, opportunity is not* — is the same truth Kayan states as **"the talent was always here; the system was not."**
- **Standards Kayan can align to:** the Global Impact Sourcing Coalition's **Impact Sourcing Standard** — a credibility marker Kayan can adopt for donors and clients.
- **Funding history:** the model was endorsed and seeded by major philanthropy (e.g., Rockefeller's Digital Jobs Africa), and is tracked by industry analysts as a growing market — useful proof points for the investor and donor narratives.

**The strategic move:** position Kayan to funders as *"impact sourcing, brought to professional services and to a conflict-affected market, with a trust guarantee no incumbent offers."*

## 2. The benchmarks — what each proves, what Kayan takes, where Kayan differs

| Benchmark | What it proved | What Kayan takes | Where Kayan stands apart |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Andela** (global tech talent) | Rigorous multi-stage vetting (~1–2% accepted) into a *certified* network; a credential carrying assessment scores + client feedback; fully-managed delivery teams as the premium tier; a flywheel from training to placement | The vetting→credential→managed-delivery spine; the Card as a record of proven work; "managed team" as the premium offer | Andela is remote-only, tech-only, VC-funded, placeless, no payment guarantee, no civic arm. Kayan is **place-based, multi-disciplinary, escrow-guaranteed, civically rooted** |
| **Gaza Sky Geeks** + oPt ecosystem (fragile-context) | In conflict markets the real failures are **payment** (broken rails, middlemen, high fees) and **client trust in commitment**; coworking hubs become economic lifelines (solar, connectivity); train-to-hire pathways work | The fragile-context realism; the Hub as infrastructure not amenity; train-to-hire (Forge); women's economic inclusion through flexible work | That ecosystem trains people to freelance *alone* on Upwork — exposed when trust or infrastructure fails. Kayan is the **institution that contracts, guarantees payment, and stands behind delivery** — the missing layer above the freelancer |
| **Impact sourcing** (Sama, DDD, iMerit) | A commercial, *less donor-dependent* social enterprise can scale and sustain; a career ladder (annotator → team lead → PM) retains and dignifies talent; gender inclusion is a design choice | The social-enterprise sustainability logic (commercial revenue funds the mission); the Trust Ladder as a real career progression; dignified-work and gender standards | Incumbents do mostly low-complexity data work, globally/remotely, and have been criticised for thin standards. Kayan does **higher-value professional work, locally, to a published trust and dignity standard** |

**One-line synthesis:** Andela gives Kayan the *engine*, Gaza Sky Geeks gives Kayan the *fragile-context truth*, impact sourcing gives Kayan the *category and the sustainability model*. Kayan's originality is the **combination no one else has assembled**: verified talent + guaranteed managed delivery + a physical trust-hub + a civic arm, in Aden.

## 3. The differentiation Kayan must own (the moat)

1. **The guarantee.** No incumbent holds the client's money in escrow and stands behind the outcome. In a market where payment is the #1 broken thing, the guarantee *is* the product.
2. **Place + trust.** A real institution with a building, a credential issued in person, and formal banking — credibility you can visit, in a market starved of it.
3. **Multi-disciplinary, higher-value.** Not data-labelling; design, software, content, research, advisory — work that pays more and dignifies more.
4. **The civic flywheel.** A commercial engine that funds a civic mandate, and a civic mandate that feeds the commercial bench — sustainable exactly when donor money is scarce.
5. **Conflict-context mastery.** Built for Aden as it is (power, FX volatility, AML scrutiny, informal economy) — a competence that is itself a barrier to entry.

## 5. Social impact & SDG alignment (for the civic and donor narratives)

Kayan's model maps cleanly to the Sustainable Development Goals — to be used as a framework, not decoration:

- **SDG 8 — Decent Work & Economic Growth** (the core): dignified, paid, accountable work; formal income in an informal economy.
- **SDG 4 — Quality Education:** Forge's skilling and credentialling.
- **SDG 5 — Gender Equality:** flexible, safe, verified pathways for women (a designed-in priority, not a side note).
- **SDG 9 — Industry, Innovation & Infrastructure:** building the trust/delivery rail the market lacks.
- **SDG 10 — Reduced Inequalities:** opportunity for those the formal market excludes.
- **SDG 16 — Peace, Justice & Strong Institutions:** a credible institution in a fragile state.
- **SDG 17 — Partnerships:** the absorption layer with donors, government, and the private sector.

Impact is expressed as **integrated operational reality**, never as segmented checklists per group — inclusion is in how the institution works, not in a separate paragraph.
