Why it exists · ONE GAP, THREE LOSERS

Three people are failed by the same missing link

01 · THE GRADUATE
Trained, certified, still unhireable

A course ends, a certificate is issued, and nothing on it tells an employer what this person can actually do. The training was real; the proof was not.

02 · THE EMPLOYER
Willing to hire, unable to tell who is real

Screening costs more than the role is worth, so the safe decision is to leave the seat empty or send the work abroad. Local capability loses to local uncertainty.

03 · THE FUNDER
Money spent, impact unprovable

Attendance sheets and a photograph are not results. A funder who cannot name what changed cannot defend the next round of funding — so the next round shrinks.

The Forge is built to close all three at once.

One written standard, one independent assessment, one register a buyer can check — and one route from the classroom to a paid engagement, because the same institution runs both ends of it.

The line on a page · NOT AN ACADEMY

A talent factory, wired directly to the work

Five things separate a Forge cohort from a course. Each one is a rule the line runs on, not a promise in a brochure.

01 · REAL WORK
Built from briefs that came through the door

Exercises are drawn from work the institution has actually been asked to do, not invented cases with tidy answers.

02 · WRITTEN CURRICULUM
A curriculum for a named need

Each cohort runs to a written curriculum built for one specialisation. It is what makes a graduate worth verifying.

03 · INDEPENDENT MEASURE
Teaching never grades itself

The assessment is taken by someone who did not deliver the sessions. The result is written, reasoned, and open to a single appeal.

04 · VERIFIED, RECORDED
The output is a position, not a page

Graduates go through the same six-step verification as anyone else who registers, and take their place on the trust ladder.

05 · CONNECTED
Visible where the work is staffed

Verified graduates appear on the register that Managed Delivery builds its teams from — the shortest distance from cohort to first paid brief.

The Forge is not called an academy, and it does not sell certificates. The recognised output is a position on the trust ladder, and its reference is the register.

Verification · SIX STEPS, ONE STANDARD

Graduation is not the finish line. It is the handoff.

The Forge and the register share one standard. A graduate goes through the same six steps as anyone else who registers with Kayan, and nothing is waived because they trained here. The assessment record travels with them into the capability decision — and that decision is taken by verification officers, not by the people who taught the cohort.

STEP 01
Identity

A real, contactable person, of legal age, who has accepted the terms.

STEP 02
Documents

Identity and eligibility documents checked and held under the data policy.

STEP 03
Screening

Sanctions screening, because clients ask and the answer has to be on file.

STEP 04
Craft assessment

Competence in a named specialisation, scored against a rubric, not a marksheet.

STEP 05
Practical task

A small, realistic deliverable, time-boxed and submitted through the platform.

STEP 06
Interview

A conversation with a verification officer, then a reasoned decision on the record.

THE DECISION
There is no outcome called rejected.

A person is verified at some position, asked for something specific, told exactly what is missing, or declined for a stated reason with one route of appeal. Two working days from complete evidence to a reasoned decision.

القرار مسبب، ولكل قرار حق تظلم واحد
WHAT IT IS NOT
Verification does not allocate work.

A position records what has been evidenced about a person's capability. It is not a queue and not a guarantee. Work is assigned on fit to a specific engagement, availability, and what the client requires — and that is said to every registrant at the start and at the end.

Where the Forge sits on it

A cohort carries a person from a record to identity verified, with an assessment record that goes into the capability decision. The rungs above are climbed on delivered work. The Forge opens the door; it does not skip the rungs.

A position can lapse. It is not a punishment.

Reminders go out at ninety, thirty and seven days before expiry with the specific action required. If it lapses, the specialisation goes dormant and is reinstated by completing what was outstanding — no stigma, no re-application from zero.

Sponsors · WHERE THE MONEY SITS

Restricted money, honoured as restricted

A sponsorship funds a named cohort. It is not income on the day it arrives — it is held, and recognised only as the cohort is delivered. The same discipline Kayan applies to client funds in escrow, applied to grants: the money is honoured for what it was given for.

01 · COMMITTED
A commitment, not income

The sponsor commits to a named cohort. It is recorded as a commitment on the register and nothing is recognised yet.

02 · HELD
Received into deferred income

Funds land as a liability, restricted to the cohort they were given for. They cannot be spent on anything else, including a different cohort.

03 · RECOGNISED
Released as delivery happens

Income is recognised stage by stage as the cohort is actually delivered — never in advance of the work.

04 · REPORTED
Reported per graduate

Each graduate is named in the impact report with the position they reached and the engagement and income that followed.

A cohort, fixed before it opens

Specialisation, seat count, dates and selection criteria are set and published before applications open. A sponsor knows exactly what is being bought.

Outcomes tracked, not assumed

Employment or engagement, income earned, and the story — followed per graduate after the cohort ends, with consent, and reported on a stated cadence.

Beyond one cohort

Multi-cohort programmes for sponsors who want a series, and commissioned cohorts for an organisation training its own team.

Trainers & governance · WHO TEACHES, WHO DECIDES

Teaching is rated, and the rating decides who teaches again

A rated roster

Trainers sit on a roster with ratings. Participants rate every cohort, and the ratings decide who is invited back. Quality of teaching is measured, not assumed.

Safeguarding and dignity

A standing standard for every participant: respectful, safe and fair — with a route to raise a concern that does not run through the trainer.

Attendance and completion

Both are tracked, and support goes to whoever falls behind. A cohort's value is in who finishes ready, not who enrolled.

Trainers grown from graduates

The strongest graduates are brought back as mentors and assessors. The roster deepens from inside the register rather than importing it.

GOVERNANCE · CONFLICT OF INTEREST
The Forge Lead is a partner, and the boundary is written down.

The role is operational within the Forge only: a standing conflict-of-interest disclosure, no cross-approval on decisions outside this line, and compensation set at arm's length. Trainers are contracted at arm's length on the same basis.
