The controlled vocabulary of what people do, how it is coded, how it is navigated, and how it grows

This document defines its structure, its coding rules, how a person navigates it in under three steps, and how it is governed as it grows.

1. Why a taxonomy is a strategic asset
A taxonomy looks like an administrative convenience. It is not. Whoever defines the categories in a market defines how everything in that market is compared, searched, priced and reported.
For Kayan the taxonomy does five jobs simultaneously: it is how a professional describes themselves at registration; it is what routes them to the right assessment; it is how a pod&apos;s combined capability and its gaps are seen; it is how a client expresses what they need; and it is the axis on which every report about supply, demand and absorption is aggregated. A weak taxonomy degrades all five at once. [JUDGEMENT]
2. Design principles
Principle
What it means
What it rules out
Inclusive of all work
Digital work, professional services, engineering, health, agriculture, logistics, skilled trades, crafts and hospitality sit in one structure with equal standing
A technology-centric taxonomy that quietly tells a tailor, a solar technician or a nurse that Kayan is not for them
Describes work, not job titles
A node is a kind of work someone does, not a position someone holds
Nodes such as Senior Manager or Consultant, which describe seniority or employment, not capability
Assessable by construction
A node may only go active once a verification method and evidence type exist for it
Nodes that sound impressive and cannot be verified — which would hollow out the trust asset
Permanent codes
A code is issued once, never reused, never re-pointed
Renumbering, which silently invalidates every historical record and every issued verification
Extensible without limit
999 nodes per group, unlimited groups, unlimited families
A fixed count such as 441, which becomes a ceiling the moment reality exceeds it
Navigable in three steps
Search, guided browse and plain-language entry, none more than three steps deep
Presenting a person with a list of several hundred items and calling it choice
Bilingual by requirement
A node is not shown in the Arabic journey until it carries an approved Arabic label
English-only categories in a market that works in Arabic

4. Navigation design
No person browses 483 items. The registration interface therefore never presents the taxonomy as a list. Three entry routes are offered simultaneously, and every route reaches a node in at most three steps.
Route
How it works
Who it serves
Search first
A single box searching node names, group names, common synonyms and tool names. Typing a tool name returns the node it belongs to.
The majority, who know exactly what they do
Guided browse
Family, then group, then node. Each level shows a plain-language description and a count.
People exploring, or unsure which family their work sits in
Plain language
Begins from how a person would describe themselves — I build websites, I fix air conditioners, I translate documents — and maps to the nodes behind it.
People whose work has no formal name, and early-career registrants
The plain-language route matters more than it appears. A skilled tailor does not think of her work as CR-AC-001. If the only way in is formal vocabulary, the taxonomy quietly excludes exactly the people the institution exists to include. [JUDGEMENT]
4.1 Selection rules
One primary node. This determines the craft assessment set.
Up to four secondary nodes, each independently assessable, at any time.
A synonym that resolves to a node is recorded as the search term used, so the synonym list improves from real behaviour.
A person who cannot find their work may submit a proposal directly from the picker; it enters the change control process at step 1 and they are notified of the outcome.
The last rule turns a dead end into an intelligence source. Every unmatched search is evidence of a gap in the taxonomy or in our language. [JUDGEMENT]

6. Governance
Eight steps, owned and recorded. The full procedure is in the workbook&apos;s Change Control sheet.
Proposal — anyone may propose, with a reason and a real example of the work.
Screening — checked for duplication and for whether it is genuinely distinct work rather than a tool or a job title.
Assessment readiness — a verification method and evidence type must exist before the node can go active.
Approval — approved, merged into an existing node, or declined with a stated reason.
Coding — next available number in the group. Never reused.
Arabic label — required before the node appears in the Arabic journey.
Release — published in a dated version; anyone holding a merged node is re-pointed automatically and notified.
Retirement — marked Retired, never deleted. Existing holders keep their verification; the node is hidden from new selection.
The taxonomy is versioned as a whole and released on a fixed cycle rather than changed continuously, so that any report can state which version it was aggregated under.
