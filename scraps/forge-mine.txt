##### 00 · READ ME #####
HOW THIS WORKBOOK WORKS
What a line workbook is | One workbook per business line, reasoned all the way through: what it sells and to whom, how it earns, how each transaction is booked, who runs it, the process and controls, and where it can be automated. The Forge is the talent factory — it builds the people the whole system depends on.
The accumulating ledger | Building the Forge surfaces needs for other domains — how sponsor money is booked, roles to hire, the handoff Tech must build from graduate to verified talent, agreements Legal must write. Those land in tab 08 and accumulate.
Numbers are placeholders | Every contested figure is an illustrative placeholder in a blue input cell, pending the finance session. The model computes so the logic is visible — no number is decided.
The reader | Internal and candid about sponsor dependency and the hard part — turning training into real income. External language is handled in the brand; the Forge is never called an &apos;Academy&apos;.
Where it connects | The Forge does not end at graduation. Graduates enter the verification pipeline and, when they pass, join the Verified Talent Map as T1 — and become available to Managed Delivery. Training that does not lead to work is not the goal; training that leads to verified, engaged talent is.


##### 01 · LINE ON A PAGE #####
Kayan Forge — the line on a page
What it is | Structured capability-building: cohorts that take people from potential to verified, work-ready talent — and then connect them to actual work through the rest of Kayan.
Who it is for | Aspiring and early-career talent who need real skills and a path to income. Sponsors and funders who want to build Yemeni capability with measurable results. And the market that needs skilled, verified people.
The promise | Real skills, independently verified, with a genuine path to work — not a certificate that leads nowhere. You leave the Forge on the talent map, not just with a page.
What it sells | Sponsored cohorts (the core, donor-aligned), enrolment where participants pay, and custom or corporate training commissioned by an organisation — plus multi-cohort sponsor programmes.
The value flow | Recruit → enrol → deliver → assess → graduate → verify (the six-step pipeline) → join the talent map as T1 → be engaged through Managed Delivery. Sponsors fund it; graduates carry it into income; outcomes and stories prove it.
Why it wins | It does not just train — it verifies and connects to work. Graduates enter the Verified Talent Map, and their income and stories become the proof a funder needs. It is the absorption layer that turns development funding into capability that lasts.
Where it sits | The talent factory. It feeds Managed Delivery with verified people, gives the Hub cohorts to run, and gives Kayan for Yemen and its sponsors measurable impact — employment, income, and stories.

##### 06 · QA & CONTROLS #####
Quality assurance & controls
Curriculum standard | Every cohort runs to a written curriculum built for a real need, not a generic syllabus. It is what makes a Forge graduate worth verifying.
Trainer quality | Trainers are on a roster with ratings; participants rate every cohort, and ratings decide who teaches again. Quality of teaching is measured, not assumed.
Assessment integrity | Assessment is against a clear standard and recorded. A Forge pass has to mean something, because it feeds directly into verification.
The verification handoff | Graduation is not the end. Graduates enter the six-step verification pipeline — identity, KYC, sanctions, skills, assessment, interview — and only those who pass join the talent map as T1. The Forge and the registry share one standard.
Outcome tracking | What matters is what happens after: employment or engagement, income earned, and the story. These are tracked per graduate and per cohort — the real measure of the Forge, and the proof a sponsor needs.
Safeguarding | A standing safeguarding and dignity standard for every participant — respectful, safe, and fair throughout.
Attendance & completion | Attendance and completion are tracked; a cohort&apos;s value is in who finishes ready, not who enrols.

##### 09 · PAIN POINTS & IDEAS #####
Pain points & ideas
What hurts and what could earn more. Candid, because it is internal.
Type | Item | So what — the response | Priority
Pain | Sponsor dependency — the core revenue rests on funders | Build a sponsor pipeline and multi-cohort programmes; add enrolment and corporate training so no single sponsor is load-bearing. | HIGH
Pain | Graduate-to-work conversion — the hardest and most important part | The whole design answers it: verification plus Managed Delivery mean a graduate can enter real, paid work — and that outcome is tracked, not assumed. | HIGH
Pain | Trainer availability and quality | A rated roster and mentoring model; ratings decide who returns; grow trainers from strong graduates. | MED
Pain | Participant follow-through and completion | Selection for commitment, mentoring, and attendance tracking; a place is earned and supported. | MED
Pain | Measuring real impact credibly | Outcome tracking — employment, income, stories — per graduate; this is what makes the Forge fundable and defensible. | MED
Idea | Multi-cohort sponsor programmes | Move sponsors from one cohort to a series — steadier revenue and deeper impact. | IDEA
Idea | TEVT accreditation | Recognised accreditation adds credibility, unlocks funding, and makes certificates count. | IDEA
Idea | Employer pathway partnerships | Agreements where employers commit to hire or engage graduates — a guaranteed path to work. | IDEA
